One of the most frequent questions about iPhone photography is about camera apps: Which is the best? The answer to this question is complex. It depends on many factors, like the type of photography you plan to do. In my case, it’s travel photography.

This yearly updated blog post about the best iOS camera apps in 2026 is intended as a starting point for you. For each camera app, I’ll highlight the top three reasons why the app is on this list and link to a more detailed review.

Use this list as a starting point to help you find your favorite camera apps. I usually update this list once a year in December or January.

## My requirements for iPhone camera apps

I have tried many camera apps for iOS since I turned to iPhone photography in 2012. I am looking for features that enable me to use the photography techniques I've been using with my DSLR, which will help me overcome the hardware limitations of the iPhone camera.

The requirements for camera apps I use and recommend have mostly stayed the same since 2012. For my travel photography with iPhone, I need the following:

1. DSLR-like manual controls and priority modes for shutter speed and ISO.
2. A way to shoot well-lit and low-noise photos in low light and at night.
3. Save photos in RAW format for better results when editing them.
4. Taking long-exposure photos of fireworks, light trails, or waterfalls.
5. An automatic mode for quickly taking snapshots.

Each recommended camera app in this list works with the latest iPhone model. In my case, that’s the iPhone 17 Pro Max with iOS 26.

## My selection of the best iPhone camera apps

My recommendations for the best camera apps in this list come from several years of experience using and testing them thoroughly. The iOS camera apps are on this list for a reason. They support my kind of travel photography with my iPhone.

### Best free iOS camera app is the stock iPhone camera app

The stock iPhone app was originally a great camera app for snapshots, and it was easy to use. But over time, Apple added more and more professional features. That makes it the best free iPhone camera app with features like:

* Apple ProRAW for shooting RAW photos
* A Night Mode that you can use with all lenses since iOS 15
* Take 48-megapixel photos using the Apple ProRAW format (iPhone 14 and up)
* Portrait Mode (which I use rarely)
* Live Photos that capture three seconds of motion. You can turn these into long-exposure photos.
* Manual exposure compensation

### The best iPhone camera app for professional control is the ProCamera App

ProCamera App has been on this list of the best iPhone camera apps since 2014. Since then, the developers have introduced excellent features you wouldn't find in any other camera app. That makes it the best iPhone camera app for professional control.

Listing all the features of the ProCamera app would fill a few pages. So I picked the three most essential features from my point of view on why this is one of the best DSLR-like camera apps for iPhone:

1. Supports DSLR-like manual settings for ISO and shutter speed, and supports manual and priority modes. You manually set the shutter or ISO speed in priority mode, and the camera app will choose the other parameter.
2. It has several shooting modes, including classic photo mode, low light mode, and HDR mode. The HDR mode is highly configurable and allows you to choose how many photos the ProCamera app should take, and you can even adjust the exposure bracketing manually.
3. It supports saving photos in classic RAW and Apple ProRAW formats. It even allows you to save 48-megapixel images from your iPhone as JPEG, which the built-in camera app doesn't offer.

In addition to this list, there are many more features that ProCamera offers, like videos, bothies, selfies, different grids as composition assistance, or automatic perspective correction.

To learn more about why I believe that the ProCamera app is the best DSLR-like camera app, check my [ProCamera App Review](/blog/procamera-app-review) in the blog. I've also written reviews about a few unique features of the ProCamera app, like the [automatic photo perspective correction](/blog/review-auto-perspective-correction-in-procamera-app) and the [ProCamera low light modes](/blog/review-low-light-modes-procamera-app). If you're already convinced, [download the ProCamera app from the App Store](aff:procamera-app).

### Best camera app for taking long exposure photos is the Slow Shutter Cam app

Slow Shutter Cam App has been on my list of the best iPhone camera apps since 2012 because it does one thing and does it well: allowing you to take [long exposure photos with iPhone](/blog/long-exposure-iphone). I bought it in 2012 and have been a user ever since then.

There are numerous reasons why I recommend the Slow Shutter Cam app, but the top 3 reasons why I believe it's the best camera app for taking long-exposure photos are:

1. It has three dedicated shooting modes for capturing light trails, motion blur, and low-light photography.
2. You can manually set the exposure time to anything between 0 and 30 seconds, but it also supports a bulb mode if you need a longer exposure time.
3. The app allows you to manually set ISO, which is essential for taking long exposure photos at night. Using a low ISO will reduce noise.

To learn more about all the features and why I believe it's the best camera app for long-exposure photography, please head over to my [Slow Shutter Cam App review](/blog/review-slow-shutter-cam-app) or [download the Slow Shutter Cam app from the App Store](/blog/review-slow-shutter-cam-app).

To use the Slow Shutter Cam app, you'll need a tripod. I’ve listed the tripods I use in my article about the [best iPhone photo accessories](/blog/recommended-iphone-photo-accessoires).

### Best camera app for long exposure photography without a tripod is the Reeheld Camera App

I discovered the Reeheld camera app in 2023 and started to use it for long-exposure photography in addition to the Slow Shutter Cam app.

Reeheld Camera app allows you to take long exposure photos of up to 30 seconds without using a tripod by using artificial intelligence and optical image stabilization. I've been using the Reeheld camera app for a few months before adding it to this list, and the results blow me away.

#### Alternatives for Reeheld Camera App

I don't know many alternatives for the Reeheld camera app. If there were similar apps, I would have discovered them already. One alternative option is taking a live photo using the iOS stock camera app and converting it to a long exposure.

But even with the iPhone 15, Live Photos are limited to 3-second exposures, while the Reeheld camera app allows you to take up to 30 seconds of long exposures. Neither app requires a tripod.

## The verdict

This list of the best camera apps for 2024 is very personal: it contains the camera apps I use myself and rely on while traveling. Nevertheless, I continuously try other camera apps to see if they fit my needs better than those I already use.

If your favorite camera app is not on this list, it's because it doesn't fit my workflow or does not have any features I miss or would use for my kind of photography.

Using these three camera apps, I'm perfectly equipped for travel photography with my iPhone for photographing cityscapes and landscapes during the day, as well as for tackling difficult light situations I encounter at night or in a lost place.

Finally, if you want a recommendation for choosing a camera app:

* If you're looking for the best free camera app with all-in-one functionality, use the stock camera app. It allows you to shoot RAW, has a night mode, and allows you to take 3-second long exposures using live photos
* If you want to photograph fireworks or need an app to take long exposures on your iPhone, consider reading the details of the [Slow Shutter Cam App review](/blog/review-slow-shutter-cam-app).
* If you always wanted to take long exposures longer than 3 seconds offered by live photos, check out my detailed [Reeheld camera app review](/blog/review-reeheld-camera-app).
* If you need a camera app with professional control like manual setting of exposure time, ISO, or an anti-shake shutter release, head to my [ProCamera App review](/blog/procamera-app-review) to learn more about this camera app.
